# Counting the Harbor Ferry by Sound

![Counting the Harbor Ferry by Sound](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Steamer_Pier,_New_York_Harbor_LOC_4a26647.jpg)

The posted ferry schedule was wrong by enough minutes to matter and right by enough minutes to be trusted. That is the most dangerous kind of timetable. I spent the morning on the lee side of the pier, counting arrivals by horn, wake, and the sudden tilt of gulls leaving the pilings.

By noon the pattern was clear: the official interval was fifteen minutes, the operating interval was closer to seventeen, and the missed connection happened whenever the dock crew had to load bicycles after the commuters had already boarded.

A field atlas should preserve these small corrections. They are not complaints; they are route facts. Someone planning a transfer needs the lived interval, not the printed one.

Next visit: record the same run during rain and compare whether bicycle loading disappears, doubles, or simply moves to a different side of the boat.
