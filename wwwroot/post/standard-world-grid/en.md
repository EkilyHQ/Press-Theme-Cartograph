# The Standard World Grid at Desk Scale

![The Standard World Grid at Desk Scale](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Standard_map_of_the_World._LOC_00556407.jpg)

A gridded map does not ask to be admired first. It asks to be used. That is why it belongs in a Cartograph demo: the theme's rails, legends, and compact controls read like equipment rather than decoration.

This week I pinned four notes around the margin and treated each as a route: north for index cards, east for links, south for tags, west for the table of contents. The old map became less an illustration than a desk surface with instructions.

The most useful maps keep their second reading faster than the first. Cartograph supports that habit with dense lists, visible metadata, and a navigation rhythm that makes the page feel checkable.

Archive note: the map is not used here for current geography. It is used as a material example of scale, hierarchy, and the useful stubbornness of grids.
