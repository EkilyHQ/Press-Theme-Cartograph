# Ridge Marker 17 and the False Shortcut

![Ridge Marker 17 and the False Shortcut](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Bench_mark_on_rock.jpg)

Marker 17 is visible from the saddle, which is why people keep walking toward it too early. The direct line looks obvious from above and becomes marsh within three minutes. The official trail bends west because the old survey crew understood water better than ambition.

I walked both routes with a stopwatch. The false shortcut is shorter by 0.21 miles and slower by nine to twelve minutes depending on shoes. It also erases the one clean view of the valley, which makes the official trail not only easier but more legible.

The revised atlas note will move the warning before the elevation profile. Warnings that arrive after the map are decoration. Warnings that arrive before the decision can change a walk.

Cartograph's article view makes room for this kind of route correction: a short lead, a dense middle, and enough metadata that the reader can judge whether the note applies to their own trip.
